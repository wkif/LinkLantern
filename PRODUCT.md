# Product

## Register

product

## Users

LinkLantern serves personal productivity users, creators who share curated resources, and technically fluent users who want a faster way to collect, search, organize, and revisit links. They use it as a daily navigation surface: importing browser bookmarks, managing private and public links, searching across engines, opening frequent destinations, and shaping a personal link library that still feels easy to maintain.

## Product Purpose

LinkLantern is a modern personal link management and sharing platform. Its purpose is to turn scattered browser bookmarks and useful web resources into a calm, searchable, personal lantern: a place where users can reach important sites quickly, keep collections organized, and selectively share valuable links with others. Success means users can add, find, open, import, and publish links with minimal friction while trusting privacy states and account settings.

## Brand Personality

Warm, calm, and quietly delightful. The product should feel reliable and task-focused like a strong productivity tool, but with enough personality to make a personal link library feel owned rather than generic. Raycast is the closest interaction reference: fast, direct, and respectful of user momentum.

## Anti-references

Do not drift into template SaaS aesthetics: generic blue-purple gradients, oversized marketing cards, empty claims, or decorative hero-metric patterns. Avoid noisy portal design with too many competing modules, ad-like density, or scattered focal points. Do not become a cold admin console of gray tables and forms; LinkLantern should preserve personal warmth even in management screens.

## Design Principles

1. Fast paths first: search, open, import, edit, and share actions should stay close to the user's current intent.
2. Personal lantern: the interface should make a user's link library feel curated, familiar, and lightly ceremonial without slowing them down.
3. Calm over clutter: support many links and content sources through hierarchy, filtering, and progressive disclosure rather than visual noise.
4. Share with confidence: public and private states must be obvious, reversible, and consistently represented.
5. Familiar controls, memorable moments: standard product UI patterns should carry the work; delight belongs in small feedback moments and empty states.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Preserve keyboard access across search, suggestions, forms, navigation, import flows, and admin actions. Maintain AA contrast in light and dark themes, avoid relying on color alone for public/private or success/error states, provide clear labels and error messages, and respect reduced-motion preferences for decorative animations.
